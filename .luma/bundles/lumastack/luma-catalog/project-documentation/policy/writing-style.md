# Writing style

**The small set of conventions every document here follows**, so a reader learns
them once rather than once per author. A newsroom would call this a stylebook.

**Only rules with a reason belong here.** A convention that cannot say what goes
wrong without it is a preference, and a preference in a policy is something
future writers have to obey and cannot argue with. Each rule below names its
failure.

## A heading names what is beneath it, never how many

**Write "Where the convention comes from", not "Four sources of convention".**

A count tells a reader nothing they can use. It does not say what the section is
about, whether it is the one they need, or whether to keep reading — and it
spends the most valuable words in the document, the ones a skimming reader
actually sees, on a number.

**It also rots faster than anything else in a document, because it is
invalidated by the single most likely edit.** Adding an item is the normal way a
document grows. A heading that counts is wrong the moment somebody does the
expected thing, and nothing flags it: the text still reads fluently, and only a
reader who bothers to count discovers the document is lying.

**The same applies to counts in body text and in status lines.** *"Seventeen
bundles"*, *"the three tools"*, *"the shelves are empty"* — all true when
written, all silently false later. Where a number genuinely matters, let
something derive it rather than asserting it in prose.

**Name the subject instead.** It survives every edit, and it tells a reader
whether to stop.

### The exception: when the number is the subject

**A heading may carry a number when the number is what the section is about,
rather than a tally of what follows.** *"Two versions at once: usually fine,
once not"* is about the situation of holding two versions simultaneously — the
section is not a list of two things, and the number cannot drift because it is
not counting anything.

The test: **would adding a paragraph make the heading wrong?** If yes, it was a
tally. If no, the number belongs to the subject and can stay.
