# Journal

**Optional, one per sweep. Append-only.** Dated entries recording what was
noticed while sweeping — observations, half-formed patterns, things to check
later, scratch.

**Entries are added, never revised.** That is what makes it cheap to write
into: a note costs nothing to maintain afterwards. It is also what preserves
the chronology, and *when did we start noticing this* is a question only an
unedited log can answer. A conclusion that has moved on gets a **new entry
saying so**, not an edit to the old one.

## What it is for

**The space between noticed and concluded.** A finding routes out at the slice
that produced it; a learning goes in the sweep once it is settled. **Neither
holds a suspicion that spans three slices and is not yet either.**

Typical entries: a pattern starting to show across files, a term that may be
retired and nothing enforces, a claim to verify when the right file comes up, a
question the sweep raised and did not answer.

## It is not a place to defer routing

**This is the failure to watch for**, because it is the exact pile the sweep's
routing rule exists to prevent — *a heap of to-be-filed-later items is filed by
nobody, because the reasoning that made each one worth capturing is gone within
a day.*

**A journal entry is a working note, not a queued action.** Anything actionable
goes to its real destination at the slice that produced it. If an entry is
still sitting here three slices later and is actionable, it was routed wrongly
rather than journalled correctly.

## Temporary by design, and harvested at close

**Nothing in a journal is meant to survive it.** It is scratch — cheap to write
into while the work is happening, and never the final home of anything.

**The close harvests it.** Every entry becomes one of three things:

| | goes to |
| --- | --- |
| something to do | **the backlog** — an idea, a finding, whatever the project uses |
| something learned | **the sweep's learnings**, where a second sweep will read it |
| neither | **dropped**, deliberately and on the record |

**This is what makes the whole sweep disposable.** The rule that nothing worth
keeping stays in a sweep needs somewhere cheap to put a half-formed thing in
the meantime — without it, an observation is either routed prematurely, which
files it badly, or lost. **The journal is how that rule is kept, not an
exception to it.**

**So a journal that was never harvested is a sweep that cannot be safely thrown
away.** The close is not optional once there is one.

## No fields

**Dates and authorship come from the core fields**, and nothing else about a
journal needs declaring. It is prose with a shape, not a record with a
contract.
